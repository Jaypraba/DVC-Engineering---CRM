The client came to us with a basement scheme priced by two other contractors.
Both had quoted for the dig. Neither had asked what the additional room was
actually for.

## What the site visit found

At 4.6m wide, the house could not absorb a basement without losing the lower
ground to plant and circulation. The interior designer on the visit made that
case in ten minutes with a tape measure. The engineer confirmed the retaining
condition against the neighbouring party wall would have driven the underpinning
cost well past the client's ceiling.

The report proposed something less dramatic: relocate the staircase, remove two
non-structural partitions, and take the lower ground back to a single space
opening onto the garden. Roughly a third of the basement budget, and more usable
room than the dig would have delivered.

## What we built

A relocated staircase with a new opening in the ground-floor structure, a
re-planned lower ground, and a full services replacement. Eleven months, no
extension of time.
