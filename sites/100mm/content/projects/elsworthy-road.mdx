The house had been refurbished twice before, both times badly. Services ran
where they were easiest rather than where they belonged, the rear addition had
settled by nearly 40mm, and a previous contractor had removed a section of
spine wall without any evidence of a supporting beam.

## What the site visit found

Our first visit established three things in two hours. The spine wall removal
was real and undocumented, which meant Building Control regularisation before
anything else could proceed. The client's plan to open the whole rear elevation
was structurally possible but would have needed piled foundations to the flank
wall, moving the cost band by roughly £180,000. And the basement they had been
quoted for elsewhere would never have repaid itself on this plot.

The written report set out all three, with a cost band for each route. The
client chose the option that kept the flank wall, spent the saving on the
interior, and did not open a basement.

## What we built

A single 6.4m steel opening across the rear, a modest extension that follows
the existing building line rather than fighting it, and a complete strip-out
and rebuild of everything behind the plaster. Structural design by DVC
Engineering. Party wall awards with two neighbours, both agreed before the
programme started.

## Where the programme went

Fourteen months, against a fifteen-month programme set at contract. The month
came back from sequencing the party wall notices during design rather than
after it — the single most common reason projects of this size run late.
