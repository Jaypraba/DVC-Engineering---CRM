Basements are the most reliable way to spend a large amount of money in London
and the least reliable way to add value. Whether one is worth doing is usually
settled by three measurements, all of which can be taken on a first visit.

## Plot width

Below about five metres, a single-storey basement loses so much of its area to
stairs, lightwell, plant and structure that the usable room left over is often
smaller than a room you could have made upstairs by moving two partitions. The
dig still costs what a dig costs.

## Retained height

The depth you are retaining sets the underpinning sequence, and the underpinning
sequence sets most of the cost. The difference between retaining 2.4m and 3.2m
is not thirty per cent more money. It is frequently double, because it changes
the pin depth, the temporary works and the amount of time the neighbouring
structure spends being propped.

## Distance to the neighbour

Excavating within three metres of an adjoining foundation, to a depth below it,
triggers the Party Wall Act, and it changes the engineering. Within one metre it
changes it substantially. Both are measurable with a tape on day one, and both
belong in the decision before a designer is appointed rather than after.

## Why this belongs in a first visit

Because all three are cheap to establish and expensive to discover late. A
client who learns in month six that the basement was never viable has usually
spent £40,000 finding out.
